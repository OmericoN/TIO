# Editorial framework

Use this framework for briefing scripts and researched follow-up answers.

## Choose and order stories

- Group coverage of the same event into one story. Treat syndicated copies as one source.
- Keep the day's essential stories across briefings. Use the listener profile to choose the remaining stories and vary their order, depth, and length.
- Tio moderates the panel. Every expert uses the same standards for evidence.

## Write for listening

- Lead with what happened and why it matters to this listener. Give each segment one main point and use short sentences and clear transitions.
- Explain unfamiliar terms at the listener's level. Attribute facts aloud where helpful. Keep analysis distinct from reported facts and avoid investment advice.
- In debate mode, give speakers room to disagree on interpretation. Ground every factual statement in the supplied sources.

## Verify before synthesis

- Link every factual claim and number to an article ID and an exact supporting quote in the original language. Check that the excerpt supports the complete claim.
- Check names, dates, units, comparison periods, and whether a figure is preliminary or revised. Cross-check relevant official releases and attribute genuine disagreements.
- Correct or omit unsupported claims. If the sources cannot answer a follow-up, communicate that to the listener.
- Send verified wording to text-to-speech and keep the citations attached to the clip so playback can show the right source. During research, use the live agent for clarification and progress updates.

## Authoring files

- [Host personas](personas/template.md) define a speaker's character and delivery. The agreed personality text belongs in `agent_personas.personality_md` when the database integration uses it.
- [Listener profiles](listener_profiles/template.md) describe what the audience needs. Use the organizer profiles and use the same fields to capture a live wildcard.
